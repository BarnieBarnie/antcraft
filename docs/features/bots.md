# Bots

Use a Bot Core on a block to activate a bot there. Bots pick up items into a 27-slot inventory. They fight hostile mobs, creepers by kiting (see [Fighting creepers](#fighting-creepers)), but flee at low health instead (see [Reflexes](#reflexes)). A hurt bot (under its full health) running a script of its own starts no fight with a mob it sees, leaving that to the script, such as one that retreats home to heal; it still fights back a mob that hurts it. A neutral mob, an enderman, zombified piglin or piglin, the warden or the wither, a bot takes on only once it hurts the bot (#750). A script can read the bot's health with [`health()`](../reference/bot/health.md), its food with [`food()`](../reference/bot/food.md), and start a fight with [`attack(entity)`](../reference/combat/attack.md) or, with a bow and arrows, [`shoot(entity)`](../reference/combat/shoot.md), which hit hostile mobs and untamed, unnamed animals; players, other bots and tamed or named animals only where the config allows it (`botsAttackPlayers`, `botsAttackBots`, `botsAttackTamed`).

Bots find their own way: they walk, jump, swim, drop up to 3 blocks, or farther where that costs less than another way down: a fatal fall costs a death, the respawn and the walk from home back to where it fell, as [`lib.cost.death`](../reference/modules/lib/cost.md#deathat-startnone) counts it (#682), and the health a fall that is not fatal takes costs the ticks its food heals it in (see [Hunger](#hunger)): 10 a point while full with saturation, then 80 a point down to 18 food, but each point at most its share of a death, which is also what a point its food does not heal costs (#708). Each point healed also costs the food it uses, 1.5 points of food or saturation at a flat 5 ticks each (#730). On peaceful, where a bot regenerates a point every 20 ticks and its food refills for free, each point costs only the ticks it takes to heal: 10 while full with saturation, then 20, and no food (#738). So a bot whose home is near, or that is well fed, jumps off a pillar rather than dig its way down, a hungry one far from home digs down, and one left on a floating platform of its own scaffold drops off (#674). A bot whose home is in another dimension counts the walk back as 6000 ticks, the time its drops lie before they despawn, so it drops only where there is no other way down (#688). A bot that would not come back from a death (`botRespawn` off, or no home) never drops farther than 3 blocks, or 8 into water. They dig through blocks in the way (keeping what they dig), and bridge or pillar with scaffold blocks (dirt, cobblestone and similar, see the `antcraft:scaffold_blocks` item tag) from their inventory, breaking a torch, sapling or plant where a scaffold block goes with its drop. A path places no more scaffold blocks than the bot carries, so one with 2 walks the long way round rather than plan 3 pillars and run out halfway (#749). Blocks it digs through that drop a scaffold block it may place, such as dirt or grass (or stone, with a pickaxe), count back, as the bot picks them up: one with 1 dirt digs out through 2 and pillars twice (#771), and one with none digs dirt to pillar out of a pit (#778). A path plans each step on the blocks as its steps before left them, so it never stands on a block it broke on the way, such as the one over its head on a climb up through a giant spruce's crown, which the bot fell into (#687), nor walks through one it placed. Nor does it climb a ladder whose block behind it it broke, or a vine none of whose faces still holds: each face holds on to the block beside it or, as in vanilla, to the vine above with that face, so a vine on a block it broke drops with it unless another face holds (#714, #726, #735). Weeping and cave vines hang from the block above, through the vines above, and twisting vines and scaffolding stand on the block below, through those below, so they drop with the block that holds them too (#752). A bot slides down anything it climbs, so climbing down a column of vines that ends in the air, or letting go of its bottom vine, is a fall off its end, costed as any drop: a path takes it only where it would take that drop (#714, #726). A path that cannot reach its goal ends where the bot stands, not hanging on vines (#726). While it digs or places a block for its next step hanging on a ladder or vines, it holds on rather than slide down, and in a vine grown where it pillars since it planned, it climbs until it can place its block (#736). A bot whose body hangs over the edge of its block into the one it bridges steps back to the middle of its own block first, as a player's body keeps them from placing a block where they stand (#656). A bot that lands over the edge of a block, its feet in the air beside it, as after overshooting a drop, counts itself on the block it rests on, so it plans from there rather than walk off into the air (#673). They walk on any block as wide as a full one, even a lower one such as farmland, a dirt path, soul sand, mud, a chest, a bed, snow two layers deep or more, or a bottom slab, as on a full block, and on stairs, but not on leaves with no log in range, which decay under them (#673); where a jump falls short of the block one higher, such as from a slab, a bed or thin snow up onto a full block, from thin snow onto a slab, or from honey, which halves a jump, they step down and up elsewhere rather than jump or pillar. A bot with Jump Boost plans its jumps with the boost, so with Jump Boost II it steps up from honey onto a block (#665). They never break chests or other block entities, or blocks next to lava or water, never plan through lava, fire, a cactus or a sweet berry bush, keep off the blocks beside a cactus, which hurts a bot that brushes it, where going round costs less than the point of hurt a brush does, costed as a fall's (a fed bot goes some 3 blocks round, one that heals slowly some 17; #830, #844), and leave a block beside such a hazard only from its middle, so their body never cuts into it at a corner, as it did off the edge of a block where a walk ended (#845); one that slid on from a walk that ended mid-step into the block beside where its next walk starts plans that walk again from where it is, rather than head across the corner between them (#848), and walk round what players build with (glass, fences, walls, doors) rather than through it. They walk round nether portals too, and never dig at a portal's frame: only `travel` takes a bot through one.

## Hunger

Bots get hungry and heal as players do (#702), with a player's food level (0 to 20), saturation and exhaustion, saved with the bot. Work adds exhaustion as it does for a player: 0.05 a jump, 0.005 a block mined, 0.1 a blow that lands, 0.01 a block swum, the exhaustion of the damage taken, and the Hunger effect's; walking costs nothing, as for a player, and bots never sprint. Every 4 exhaustion takes a point of saturation, or with none left a point of food (not on peaceful). A hurt bot at 20 food with saturation heals every 10 ticks, at 18 food or more a point every 80 ticks, each heal costing exhaustion; at 0 food it starves, losing a point every 80 ticks down to 10 health on easy, 1 on normal, and to death on hard. The `naturalHealthRegeneration` game rule turns healing off as for players. On peaceful, with that rule on, a bot regenerates as a player does: a point of health and of saturation every second, and a point of food every half second (#730). The Saturation effect feeds a bot as it does a player (#730). A bot starts and [respawns](death.md) with 20 food and 5 saturation.

A script reads the bot's food with [`food()`](../reference/bot/food.md) and [`saturation()`](../reference/bot/saturation.md) and eats with [`eat(item)`](../reference/inventory/eat.md), which takes the item's eat time (1.6 seconds for most food) and gives its nutrition, saturation and effects, such as a golden apple's. A full bot eats only what can always be eaten, such as a golden apple. Bots keep themselves fed with [`lib/food`](../reference/modules/lib/food.md) (#703): the long-running default jobs and colony scripts call its `upkeep()` each round, which eats carried food when hungry and, carrying none, takes some from the chests or the Colony Core, or makes bread from wheat or cooks raw meat, hunting cows, pigs, sheep or chickens for it where the chests hold none (#719). So a colony needs a farmer. A script of your own that works long calls `lib.food.upkeep()` in its loop. The bundled [`jobs.guard`](../cookbook/guard.md) fights the hostile mobs near home and, hurt, walks home, eats and heals before it fights again (#705).

`botHunger = false` (config) turns it off: food and exhaustion stay as they are, and bots neither heal nor starve, as before hunger. It costs the server little: a few sums a bot a tick (`bot.food` in `/antcraft profile`).

## Fighting creepers

A bot fights a creeper by kiting, with what it holds, so it needs no bow or arrows (#706). A creeper's fuse lights once its target is within 3 blocks and goes out only once that target is more than 7 blocks away. While a creeper within 8 blocks of the bot has its fuse lit, the bot strikes it only if its weapon is ready and the fuse has burnt less than about a third; otherwise it backs off to the spot that takes it furthest from the nearest creeper within 16 blocks, lit or not, for each block walked, so between two creepers or against a wall it backs off to the side, not into the other creeper or the wall (#725), by a way that never takes it more than a block nearer the creepers than it stands, not one that ends further off but runs past the creeper (#785), until it is more than 7 blocks off, and waits there until the fuse is out, then steps in and strikes again. It steps in only from within 3 blocks and with no other hostile mob within 3 blocks of it, which would stand in its way there and back (#717); once such a mob kept it from stepping in, it does not turn back to step in when the mob moves on while the fuse burns, rather than flip between the two every few ticks (#793). So a creeper takes a blow each time it comes at the bot, and dies without exploding: with an iron sword, in about four. This holds in its own fights and in [`attack`](../reference/combat/attack.md), which waits while the bot backs off, and against any lit creeper near it, such as one its sword's sweep hit beside another mob. A bot cornered where no spot it can walk to takes it further off, such as at the end of a 1-wide tunnel, goes at the creeper and strikes it whenever its weapon is ready, rather than stand still (#733), but only within 6 blocks of the creeper, where its blast reaches: further off, it stays where it is rather than walk into the blast (#785). From more than 3 blocks off it goes at it only while the fuse has burnt less than about a third: with the fuse further gone it would get near only as the creeper explodes, where the blast hurts most, so it stays at the edge of the blast, which hurts little there (#793). There it cannot back off 7 blocks, and a sword does not kill a creeper within its fuse of a second and a half, so the blast still catches it: accepted, as a death costs only what the bot carries, rather than dig or pillar out of the blast. A bot that kited out of a lit creeper's range takes it for no target until its fuse is out, so it does not walk back into the blast (#733). At flee health the bot kites a lit creeper too: the flee reflex still holds it, so its script waits, but yields its movement to kiting while the fuse burns, and every other reflex, such as stepping out of fire, comes before kiting (#725).

With a bow or crossbow and arrows, a script can fight from range instead: [`shoot(entity)`](../reference/combat/shoot.md) keeps 6 to 16 blocks from the mob, draws, aims above it for the arrow's drop and ahead of it for its movement, and fires real arrows as a player would, about one a second with a bow, until the mob dies (#707). Three arrows kill a creeper before it comes near enough to light its fuse, and [`attack`](../reference/combat/attack.md) on a creeper shoots it this way when the bot carries a bow and arrows. So does the bot's own fight with a hostile mob it targets, rather than kite or strike it, while it carries a bow or crossbow and arrows; with none, it kites a creeper as above and strikes other mobs with its best weapon (#724). Not a skeleton, which outshoots it and is better closed in on, nor an enderman, which arrows do not hit: those it fights in melee. A bot shooting under 12 health does not walk closer to a mob out of its range, but waits for it to come (a scratch does not stop it, #750); one it cannot get nearer to, it tries a way to again only once a second, not every tick (#792); it backs off from one too near as before. When the bow is put away for a blow, such as kiting's strike at a lit creeper or once the arrows run out, the bot takes out its best melee weapon first (#734). A bot's arrows pass through every other bot, whoever owns it and whatever its colony, as bots only shoot mobs: a bot in the line of fire is not hurt, and the arrow flies on to the mob (#775). Players and other mobs in the way are still hit.

## Reflexes

**Reflexes** run every tick before the script and cannot be turned off: a bot steps out of fire and lava, swims up when its air runs low (`botSurfaceAir`), steps out from under falling blocks, and at or below `botFleeHealth` it flees a hostile mob near it, home or away, striking back only at a mob in its reach. An action a reflex cuts short ends with `interrupted`.

Reflexes run every tick in Java, before the script, and cannot be turned off by a script:

- the pathfinder never plans through lava or fire, and a fall that hurts only where it costs less than another way (#682);
- in fire or lava, or next to flowing lava: step aside, to the nearest block within 4 where it can stand clear of fire and lava, through the fire if need be;
- under water with less than `botSurfaceAir` air left (config, default 100 of 300 ticks): swim the shortest way, within 12 blocks, to where its head is out of the water, along under a ceiling if need be;
- at or below `botFleeHealth` health (config, default 6 of 20) with a hostile mob within 8 blocks: flee home when the bot is in its home's dimension and home lies further from the mob than the bot, and away from the mob otherwise, with its own pathfinder as `goto` does, digging or pillaring out where it must, planning again when the mob moves more than 4 blocks and waiting a second after finding no way out; a fleeing bot does not chase a mob, but strikes the one it flees while that one is in its reach (as often as in a fight, by the attack speed of its weapon), such as a mob climbing the ladders of a shaft right under it, which it cannot outrun; it does not stop on the ladders to fight such a mob, as a bot at flee health loses that fight, so it keeps climbing and striking;
- a falling block (sand, gravel) over it: step aside, out from under it.
- standing where another bot's `build` puts a block, when that build asks it to make way: step aside, to the nearest block within 4 out of the build's spots (back to the middle of its own block when only its edge is in one), for as long as the build keeps asking and a second after, or until its hold (below) ends, whichever comes first, so a bot does not make way again on its way back. A bot running `idle.py` keeps off the build's spots, standing where it stepped to, while the building bot holds the job the build was called with (`job=`, as `colony.builder` passes the `build` job it took); a build called without one holds it while the building bot holds any `build` job off its colony's job board and else until ten seconds after its last `build` call, so it does not walk back home onto the site between calls, such as while the builder fetches what it missed.

The nearer danger comes first, in the order of the list. A reflex takes over only where it has a way out: a bot walled in by fire, or under water with no air within reach, is left to its script.

When a reflex takes over, the running action ends with `interrupted`; a [`mineshaft`](../reference/mining/mineshaft.md) search, which does not move the bot, only pauses and goes on once the reflex lets go. While the reflex holds the bot the script does not run; once the danger is past, the script goes on with that result and decides what to do next. Thresholds are config options, not script options, in v1; 0 turns the drowning or the fleeing reflex off.
