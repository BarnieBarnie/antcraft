# `lib/get`

The resolver: get anything, and the tool to get it with, by the cheapest way. A module of the mod's default library: `from lib.get import get, ensure_tool`, then `get("stone_axe")` (see [Modules & import](../../../language/modules.md)). It is a script like any other, in `config/antcraft/scripts/lib/get.py`, written with [`lib/stock`](stock.md), [`lib/craft`](craft.md), [`lib/tools`](tools.md), [`lib/cost`](cost.md), [`lib/trees`](trees.md) and [`lib/dig`](dig.md).

The skills below it assume what they need: [`lib.dig.stone`](dig.md#stonen-radius32) a pickaxe, [`lib.craft.craft_here`](craft.md) the raw items. `lib/get` is where those needs are met, so a script asks for what it wants and the chain of who fetches what lives in one place (see [The script library](../../../design/library.md#the-resolver-libget)). What it takes from a chest is borrowed: end a job with [`give_back()`](borrow.md).

To keep it within an area, set [`lib.find.WHERE`](find.md#where) to a function of a [`Pos`](../../types/pos.md): every chest it takes from, every tree, stone, ore and dirt it gathers, and every cost `choose` and `Source.cost` estimate leave out the positions it is false for. It starts as `colony_edge`, so a colony member's script neither borrows from a neighbour's chest nor fells a tree outside the colony.

```python
from lib.get import get
from lib.borrow import give_back

r = get("stone_pickaxe")
if r["ok"]:
    get("cobblestone", 64)
give_back()
```

## `get(item, n=1, radius=32)`

Makes the bot carry at least `n` of `item`, an item id such as `"stone_axe"` or `"minecraft:raw_iron"` (any log for a log, such as `"oak_log"`: planks come from any). Called again, it does nothing. It tries, in turn:

1. what the bot carries: where it carries `n`, it is done;
2. the chests of [`lib.stock.chests(radius)`](stock.md#chestsradius32): it [takes](stock.md#take_up_toitem-n-radius32-chestsnone) as many as they hold, up to `n` with what the bot carries, borrowed (from the Colony Core taken), and gets only the rest by the steps below; so with 5 planks in a chest, `get("oak_planks", 8)` takes the 5 and crafts the rest from a log;
3. `SOURCES[item]`, for a raw item: it makes the bot hold the source's tool with `ensure_tool` (below), then runs the source for what is short; where the source gives a `tier` with its failure, as [`lib.dig.ore`](dig.md#oreitem-n-radius32-ynone-facingnorth-length32)'s `no_ore` does for the veins its pickaxe did not drop, it makes the bot hold a tool of that tier and runs the source once more for the rest, so a bot with a stone pickaxe that branched past diamonds gets an iron pickaxe and mines them;
4. crafting: [`lib.craft.plan(item, n)`](craft.md) lists the raw items, the crafting table and furnace included. Before it gathers any, it takes what the plan crafts or smelts on the way from the same chests, borrowed (from the Colony Core taken), the last step first, and plans again, so it gathers only what they do not cover: with spruce planks in a chest and no tree, `get("spruce_trapdoor")` borrows the planks, and with sticks in a chest a wooden pickaxe takes them rather than more planks from logs. For a step of planks where the chests hold none of the exact kind, it takes any planks once, so birch planks in a chest make a crafting table or sticks, whose recipes take any planks, though the plan picked oak (#580); ponytail: where the recipe takes only the exact kind, as an oak door does, those planks stay borrowed, at most one step's worth. It takes a step's whole output, 4 planks where the sticks need 2, and the plan made again counts the rest for later steps: with 8 planks in a chest a wooden pickaxe takes two steps of 4 and gathers nothing. Then `get` gets each raw item, chests first as above, plans again (at most 5 rounds, as getting a tool for one raw item can use up another), then [`lib.craft.craft_here(item, n)`](craft.md). A log it got as any log that the plan made again still asks for, the recipe takes only that kind, as oak slabs take oak planks: it gets that kind alone (`lib.trees.logs` with `kind`), so with only birch trees about `get("oak_slab", 6)` fails `no_logs` for the oak log rather than felling the birch round after round (#933). Before each plan and before `craft_here` it walks back to the spot where it began, where a chest or a gather took it out of reach of the crafting table or furnace that was within `lib.craft.REACH` (4) there: both look for stations within reach of where the bot stands, so from beside a far chest they planned to craft another table, and with no wood `get("bucket")` failed `no_logs` leaving a chest's iron, or not, by which of several equal paths took it to the chest (#692), or to put a furnace down, which without a pickaxe failed (#698); ponytail: a smoker or blast furnace is not walked back to. Where the plan smelts and the bot carries no pickaxe to mine the furnace back, it gets one first, unless a raw item has no source within `radius` and no chest holds it (or a step of it): then it fails at once with that source's reason, so with no animal in reach `get("cooked_beef")` fails `no_animal` and makes no pickaxe (#741, `getCookedBeefWithNoAnimalFailsWithoutMakingAPickaxe`). Where the plan wants coal, as fuel or for a torch, more than the chests within `radius` hold, it gets logs for the rest instead where they cost less than the coal source (see [`wood_for_coal`](#wood_for_coalk-radius)), smelts them into charcoal at once and plans again, so the coal it takes from the chests after is not burnt making the charcoal. So with no coal ore exposed within `radius`, a bot smelts with wood rather than dig stairs for one coal, and with 1 coal in a chest 8 torches take that coal and one charcoal (#564).

So an empty bot by a tree and some stone gets a stone axe: logs by hand, a wooden pickaxe from them, cobblestone with it, then the axe.

- **Returns** a [`result()`](result.md) dict: `ok`, or a reason with `missing`, the item to how many it lacks:

| Reason | When |
|---|---|
| `no_recipe` | the item has no source and no recipe makes it, such as an elytra, and no chest holds enough |
| `cycle` | getting the item waits on getting itself, or more than `DEPTH` (8) gets wait on each other |
| `no_tool` | no tool of the kind a source needs can be had (see `ensure_tool`) |
| another reason | that of the source, the chest or `craft_here` that failed, such as `no_logs` or `no_stone` |

Where it fails, it [gives back](borrow.md#give_backradius16-since0) what it borrowed during the call, from a chest or for a tool, so a failed `get` leaves the chests as it found them: with 5 planks in a chest and no log in reach, `get("oak_planks", 8)` takes the 5, fails for the rest and puts the 5 back. What was borrowed before the call stays borrowed, and a nested `get` (a raw item, a tool) that failed has given its own back already. What it made from borrowed items, such as planks from a borrowed log, the bot keeps; the log is struck off as used. That is on purpose: undoing a craft is not possible, and the planks are as good to the next job as the log. Where the script errors or is stopped in the middle of `get`, it gives back nothing itself: an error goes on to the caller, whose `try`/`finally` around its job gives back (as the bundled jobs do), and after a stop the records stay in `borrowed()`, kept in memory, for the next run's `give_back()`.

- **Pauses** yes, while it walks, gathers and crafts; not where the bot carries `n` already.

## `ensure_tool(kind, uses=1, tier=None, radius=32)`

Makes the bot hold a tool of `kind` (`"pickaxe"`, `"axe"`, `"shovel"`, `"hoe"` or `"sword"`) that lasts `uses` more blocks and is at least `tier` (`"wooden"`, `"stone"`, `"copper"`, `"iron"`, ...; `None`: any). With `tier="hand"` no tool is needed, as logs and dirt break by hand: it gets one only where it is cheaper than the hand. Where the carried ones of at least `tier` last, it [`hold`](tools.md)s the best at once; with `tier` `None` or `"hand"` a carried tool of no listed tier (a modded one) counts too, as it does for [`lib.tools.best`](tools.md#bestkind), but not where a tier is asked, as its tier is not known. Else, unless `tier` is `"hand"`, it takes the first tool that lasts with them in the nearest of `lib.stock.chests(radius)` that holds one, where the walk there and the [`breaking`](cost.md) of `uses` blocks with it cost no more than making a tool of the lowest tier of `lib.craft.MADE` it may (wooden for any tier), its make cost and breaking with it (#580; a fixed walk of 16 blocks before), borrowed, but from the colony's core. Where the chest tool already breaks faster than that tier, it takes it without costing the make; else it costs the make once, for the first such chest tool, and only up to that tool's cost (the `limit` of #582), with the chests of that pass, so a tool in the colony's core needs no scan; where making is cheaper, the choice goes to `options`. So an empty bot borrows an iron axe 20 blocks off for 30 logs, rather than cost the options; with logs carried, a wooden axe made beats a stone axe 30 blocks off, and the choice goes to `options`. Where no tier is asked, a chest tool of no listed tier, as a modded one, counts too, and breaks as a wooden one. Where no tool can be made for want of a raw item, it fails at once with that source's reason, `no_logs`, rather than cost the options first (4 ticks for an empty bot, 114 before, #562): where no log is within `radius` (the source's `there`) and no chest holds logs, planks or sticks, as a stick takes them; and where the sticks are covered, as carried, where each tier's tool still needs logs, for a wooden one's planks or the wooden pickaxe a stone or iron one's raw items take, as no pickaxe is carried or in a chest (13 ticks with two sticks carried, #580). Only then does it cost the ways to make one, `options(kind, uses, tier, radius)` (below, some 100 ticks in a colony), and take the cheapest way, made with `get` and held (#551). So felling a few trees takes a stone axe, a forest an iron one, and a chest's iron axe beats making either. Where a way fails it tries the next: a bot with logs and no stone in reach makes a wooden pickaxe once the stone one fails `no_stone`. A hoe's `uses` are the blocks it tills: tilling takes as long with any hoe, so only the cost of getting one counts, and a carried or chest hoe that lasts beats making one, a wooden one from logs where none does (`colony.farmer` gets its hoe this way).

- **Returns** a [`result()`](result.md) dict: `ok`, `no_tool` with `missing` where there is no way (a tier no source can get the raw items of, or one whose recipe waits on an item being got), the reason of the source of a raw item every tool needs where none is within `radius` (`no_logs`), or the reason `get` failed with for the last tier it tried to make.
- **Pauses** yes, while it walks to a chest or makes the tool; not where a carried tool lasts, or the hand is cheapest.

## `choose(kind, uses=1, tier=None, radius=32)`

The cheapest of `options(kind, uses, tier, radius)`, a tuple `(ticks, how, tier, at)`; `None` where there is none. A finder: it takes and makes nothing, so a script can ask what `ensure_tool` would do. One call costs some 46,500 instructions (26 ticks at the default budget, #595, #630) for an empty bot with no chest near, most of it [`lib.craft.plan`](craft.md) of each tier's tool, each of which asks `needs` (3000); each tier plans another item, so nothing is planned twice (#522).

```python
from lib.get import choose

choose("axe", 30)[1:3]    # ('make', 'stone')
choose("axe", 500)[1:3]   # ('make', 'iron'): it wears out four stone axes and breaks slower
```

## `options(kind, uses=1, tier=None, radius=32)`

Each way to have a tool of `kind` for `uses` blocks of at least `tier`, cheapest first, each a tuple `(ticks, how, tier, at)`:

| `how` | `tier` | `at` | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| `"hand"` | `None` | `None` | `tier` is `"hand"` |
| `"carried"` | the best carried | `None` | the carried tools of `kind` of at least `tier` last `uses` blocks together |
| `"chest"` | the tool's | the chest, a [`Pos`](../../types/pos.md) | a tool in one of `lib.stock.chests(radius)` lasts the `uses` the carried tools of `kind` of at least `tier` leave: a worn one is used up first, so with a stone pickaxe of 20 uses carried a chest's of 20 more does for 30 |
| `"make"` | one of `lib.craft.MADE` | how many to carry in all | the raw items of `plan` for that many each have a source |

`ticks` estimates the work, with the numbers of [`lib/cost`](cost.md): [`breaking`](cost.md)`(uses, material, tier)` for every way (material `"stone"` for a pickaxe, `"wood"` for an axe, `"dirt"` for a shovel, none for a hoe or sword), plus the walk to a chest, or for `"make"` the [`cost(k, radius)`](#sourcedo-toolnone-tiernone-estimatenone-orenone-nearnone-ynone-keepnone-dropsnone) of each raw item's source for the `k` that `plan` lists for as many tools as `uses` wears out, `SMELT` for each item smelted, the plan's crafting table and furnace (or a smoker or blast furnace that cooks every smelted input), each the walk to the one [`plan`](craft.md#planitem-n1-pnone) counts within `lib.craft.REACH` (4), as `craft_here` and `smelt_here` walk to it, from the last chest `get` takes from where it takes from one, as it walks back (#698; ponytail: not from a source's ore or tree), else the ticks to mine back the one `craft_here` puts down, by [`breaking`](cost.md) a `"table"` with the best axe carried and a `"furnace"` with the best pickaxe (a wooden one where none is carried, as `get` gets one before it smelts), a furnace once for each stack of 64 a step smelts, as `smelt_here` puts one down for each (#635), so a bot with the planks for a wooden pickaxe and its table takes a stone pickaxe from a chest 4 blocks off rather than make one and mine the table back by hand (#630), and the tools those sources need: for each kind, one of the highest tier any of them needs (wooden for any tier; an ore source's tier from its nearest exposed block), free where the bot carries tools of that kind and tier that last them all, else the walk to the nearest chest of [`lib.stock.chests(radius)`](stock.md#chestsradius32) with one that lasts the rest, else the make cost of one. A step of the plan those chests hold, as `get` takes it (planks of any kind for planks), costs the walk to the nearest of them, and the raw items it covers are left out: with 4 birch planks in a chest, a crafting table costs the walk there, not a log (#580). So is what they smelt: where the chests hold all the plan smelts, it costs no `SMELT`, furnace or fuel, so with three iron ingots in a chest across the area and a crafting table by the bot a bucket costs the walk to the chest and back to the table, 115 ticks (1147 before, three `SMELT` and a furnace to put down; #709, `makeCostLeavesOutTheSmeltingOfIngotsAChestHolds`), and `get` takes some 70. Where they hold part of it, the furnace counts only for the stacks left to smelt: with 64 of the 72 iron ingots of 192 iron bars in a chest, one furnace put down and mined back, not two (#729, `makeCostCountsTheFurnaceOnlyForTheSmeltingAChestLeaves`). Each raw item, the item itself where it is raw, costs the walk to the nearest chest within `radius` that holds any, as `get` takes it from there first, for as much as the chests within `radius` hold, the rest from its source, its walk from that chest: three raw iron, two of them in a chest 6 blocks off and an ore 6 past it, cost 83 ticks and took 63 (#661, #675, `makeCostOfRawIronInAChestIsCloseToItsTicks`; 180 before, when only coal counted the chests, and 90 while an ore's walk was a flat part of its estimate). For coal the rest, or all where none holds any, is the cheaper of its source and the logs [`wood_for_coal`](#wood_for_coalk-radius) would get instead, smelted, as `get` burns wood where coal is deep; with wood, coal needs no pickaxe. So an iron axe costs an empty bot a stone pickaxe for the raw iron, itself made with a wooden pickaxe (or the walk to a chest that holds one), and the trip down to iron's depth once where no iron ore is exposed within `radius`. Carried items count: a bot with the cobblestone and a log makes a stone axe for nothing but the breaking, and one with a stone pickaxe pays no pickaxe for raw iron. Measured against `get` (#635, `GetGameTest` `makeCostOfAnIronAxeIsCloseToItsTicks`: a stone pickaxe carried by a tree, coal ore and iron ore), an iron axe costs 1182 ticks (its three iron ore a vein, #661) and takes some 1360. A raw item costs its own source, as `get` gathers it: three raw iron cost 173 ticks (the walk to the nearest ore and its mining, 30 and 23, and the source's estimate, 60, which counts a walk, for each of the other two) and took 126 to 132 with an ore at each of three corners of the area (#649, `makeCostOfRawIronIsCloseToItsTicks`); before #649 `make_cost` of any raw item was `None`, as it turned down the item it was costing itself.

Where `tier` is `"hand"` or the carried tools last, neither of which can fail, a tier dearer to make than them is left out: `make_cost` stops as soon as the cost passes theirs, each raw item's source counted at its `least` before its ore scan, the raw items with no ore first (so a tier whose ore in sight makes it cheapest is kept, #681), and an ore whose pickaxe is made of ingots (copper, iron or gold) that the bot neither carries nor finds in a chest, as the iron pickaxe a diamond needs, also at a `SMELT` for each of the three ingots it lacks (#700: `make_cost("diamond", 3, limit=500)` of an empty bot scanning radius 32 ran some 35,000 instructions, now some 18,000), so for a few logs a diamond or iron axe costs no ore scan and no pickaxe (some 100 ticks less for the colony lumberjack before each tree, #582). Before it plans a tier at all it bounds it by `needs` alone, as `lib.craft.plan`'s `needs` with the crafting table and furnace costs as much again: the `SMELT`s, the stations (the walk to or the mining back of the crafting table and furnace) and each raw item's `least` as above, unless a chest holds any of its items (#710: `make_cost("iron_axe", 1, radius=8, limit=300)` of an empty bot ran 5,100 instructions, now 1,400, and `choose("axe", 30, radius=8)` 44,600, now 33,900, as it no longer plans the copper, iron and diamond axes). Within one `options` it runs `needs` and `plan` of each item and count once: a tier's tool costed again as a source's tool, as the wooden pickaxe the stone pickaxe's cobblestone needs, takes them from the first time (#728: `choose("pickaxe", 30, radius=8)` of an empty bot ran 34,500 instructions, now 28,500). Likewise it costs the tiers to make lowest first, each only up to the cheapest made so far, as a dearer tier would come after it: the lowest tier it may (wooden for any tier) is always costed in full, so it stays as the way to try where a cheaper one fails (#595).

ponytail: rough estimates, not a simulation; the uses of a carried tool of another tier do not count toward making one. One `options` call costs some 61,000 to 64,000 instructions (some 32 to 35 ticks) for an empty bot with no chest near, most of it [`plan`](craft.md) of each tool and pickaxe tier (copper, a tier since #578, added some 6,000).

## `SOURCES`

How `get` gathers each raw item: a dict of item id (`"#minecraft:logs"` for any log) to a `Source`. It starts with:

| Item | How | Tool |
|---|---|---|
| any log | [`lib.trees.logs`](trees.md) | axe, or by hand |
| cobblestone | [`lib.dig.stone`](dig.md#stonen-radius32) | pickaxe |
| dirt, sand | the exposed ones within `radius`, nearest first | shovel, or by hand |
| flint | the exposed gravel within `radius`, nearest first, then the gravel it carries or what the dug gravel dropped, put down beside its feet or head and dug again, never over its head, where it would fall on the bot (#754), until enough flint drops (one gravel in ten without Fortune, every one with Fortune III; `no_block` where it has neither) | shovel, or by hand |
| coal, raw iron, raw copper, raw gold, redstone, lapis lazuli, diamond, emerald | [`lib.dig.ore`](dig.md) | pickaxe of [`lib.tools.tier_for`](tools.md) its ore |
| each sapling of `#minecraft:saplings` but the mangrove propagule, apple | the exposed leaves of grown trees (not `persistent`) that drop it within `radius`, nearest first, broken by hand until enough drop (`no_tree` where none is left) | none, by hand |
| mangrove propagule | the ripe propagules (`age` 4) hanging from mangrove leaves within `radius`, nearest first, broken by hand until enough drop (`no_tree` where none is left) | none, by hand |
| beef and leather (cows, mooshrooms), porkchop (pigs), mutton (sheep), chicken and feather (chickens) | a `Hunt`: the animals within `radius` (at most 64), nearest first, killed with [`attack`](../../combat/attack.md) and the drops picked up (`no_animal` where none is left) | a carried sword, else by hand |

Saplings and apples come from breaking leaves (#602), with the chances of the 26.3 loot tables: one in 20 leaves drops a sapling (oak, spruce, birch, acacia, dark oak, pale oak, cherry, azalea, flowering azalea; poplar from orange, red or yellow poplar leaves), one in 40 for jungle, and one in 200 oak or dark oak leaves an apple. The estimate is that many leaves at `LEAF` (11) ticks each, 6 to break one by hand and a step. Each source's `near` is its leaves, so where none is within `radius` `get` fails `no_tree` at once. The source breaks them with [`mine(p, hand=True)`](../../blocks/mine.md), as with a bare hand, so a bot that carries shears or a silk touch tool, which would break them into leaves, still gets saplings (#609). Where it pillars up to leaves out of reach, it takes the pillar back after with [`lib.trees.take_back`](trees.md) (#654), only what its own walks placed: what the caller's walks placed before and had not taken back stands and stays listed by [`placed_scaffold`](../../movement/placed_scaffold.md), so a script standing on its own pillar keeps it (#657). It breaks only a grown tree's leaves: leaves whose `persistent` state is true are placed by a player (a hedge, a roof), and stay, so with only those in reach `get` fails `no_tree` (#609). No leaves drop a mangrove propagule: it hangs from a grown mangrove's leaves, and its source breaks those hanging propagules within `radius` that are ripe, by hand, nearest first, as only a ripe one (`age` 4) drops itself (#650). A grown mangrove has some 6 of them, at a random age, 1 or 2 of them ripe; an unripe one is left to ripen, and a planted one (not `hanging`) is a farm's and stays. With none ripe within `radius`, `get` fails `no_tree` at once.

Meat, leather and feathers come from hunting (#719), so a bot with no farm and no food in the chests eats: [`lib.food.ensure_food`](food.md) gets raw meat this way and cooks it. A `Hunt(item, mobs, per, health)` is a `Source` whose `mobs` is an entity [selector](../../types/selectors.md), `per` how many of `item` one animal drops on average (beef and porkchop 2, mutton 1.5, the rest 1) and `health` how much one has (a cow or pig 10, a sheep 8, a chicken 4). It skips baby animals, which drop nothing (an [`Entity`](../../types/entity.md)'s `baby`; `GetGameTest` `getBeefSkipsABabyCowAndKillsTheAdult`, #742). It holds a sword where the bot carries one ([`lib.tools.hold`](tools.md)) and makes none. Its `there(radius)` is whether [`entities`](../../world/entities.md) lists a grown one within `radius`, so with none in range `get` fails `no_animal` at once and `make_cost` is `None` (`GetGameTest` `getBeefFailsWithNoAnimalInRange`). Its `cost(n, radius, start)` is the walk to the nearest animal, a kill for each `per` of `n`, `SWORD_KILL` (3) ticks for each of its health with a sword carried, else `HAND_KILL` (9), one 1-damage blow each, so a cow 30 or 90 and a chicken 12 or 36 (`makeCostOfAChickenIsLessThanOfABeef`), and `BETWEEN` (40) from each animal to the next: with a stone sword a beef from a cow 6 blocks off costs 60 ticks and took 50 (`makeCostOfBeefFromACowIsCloseToItsTicks`). Each call of `there`, `cost` and the hunt's round costs the 120 instructions of `entities`, so a hungry bot with no food tries each meat for a few hundred. ponytail: an animal that cannot be got to is skipped.

An ore source's pickaxe tier comes from the block it is about to mine: before it digs, `get` finds the nearest exposed block of the source's `ore` and asks [`lib.tools.tier_for`](tools.md#tier_forwhat) of its position, which reads the block's `needs_*_tool` tags, so a modded ore that needs an iron pickaxe gets one. Where none is exposed it uses the source's `tier`. ponytail: only that first block's tier counts; a vein further on that needs more is tried with the same pickaxe.

A script adds a source for anything the library cannot get yet, or replaces one, by assigning to it; `get` uses it from then on, also for the items it crafts from it:

```python
from lib.get import get, SOURCES, Source
from lib.result import result
import lib.find

def clay(n, radius):
    return result(mine(lib.find.nearest("clay", radius)).reason)  # a clay block drops 4

SOURCES["minecraft:clay_ball"] = Source(clay, tool="shovel", tier="hand")
get("clay")   # a clay block, crafted from the 4 clay balls
```

## `Source(do, tool=None, tier=None, estimate=None, ore=None, near=None, y=None, keep=None, drops=None)`

How to gather a raw item. `do(n, radius)` gets `n` more of it within `radius` blocks and gives a [`result()`](result.md); `get` calls it with the tool held. `tool` is the kind of tool it needs (`None`: none), `tier` the lowest tier that drops the item (`None`: any; `"hand"`: the bare hand does too, so a tool is only got where it pays). `estimate` is the ticks one takes, for `options` (through `cost`); `None` gives `ESTIMATE` (100). `ore` is the block or tag of the ore it digs, such as `"#c:ores/coal"` for coal's, and `y` the height `do` digs down to for it where none is exposed, for `cost`. `near` is the block or tag `do` gets the item from, where none within `radius` means there is nothing to get (`None`: not known): the log, dirt and sand sources set it, so where the colony has no tree `get` calls `do` at once, without a tool, and it fails at once (`no_logs`) instead of after costing and making an axe or the tools a pickaxe needs (#554). `keep(p)`, where given, says whether `do` would take the block of `near` at `p`: the leaves sources take only a grown tree's leaves (`keep` is `lib.get.grown`), so persistent leaves do not count as near (#649). `drops(level)`, where given, is the mean of the item one block drops with a tool of that Fortune level, and `estimate` the ticks of one block then: its `blocks(n)` is the blocks that drop `n`, by the Fortune of the best tool of its kind carried (the `enchantments` of a [`Stack`](../../types/stack.md)), and `cost` and `least` count those (#754). The ore sources take `ore_drops`, the mean of the game's ore bonus (1, 1.33, 1.75 and 2.2 for Fortune 0 to 3), and flint the chance gravel drops it (`FLINT_CHANCE`: one in 10, 7, 4 and every one), so with a Fortune III shovel three flint cost 75 ticks and took 72 (`GetGameTest` `makeCostOfFlintIsCloseToItsTicks`). Copper, lapis and redstone drop more than one an ore (`ORE_DROPS`, #770): copper 3.5 and lapis 6.5 times `ore_drops`, redstone 4.5 and half the Fortune level more. Its `there(radius)` says whether one `keep` allows is in reach (always where `near` is `None`). `cost` does not look at it, nor `make_cost` for a raw item the item's own steps take, which it costs as if there were some (so `choose` costs a wooden axe where no tree is in sight); `make_cost` of the raw item itself is `None` where `there` is false and no chest holds any, as `get` of it then fails at once (#649), and so is that of an item whose crafting table or furnace takes a raw item the item itself does not, such as the logs of the table for the furnace an iron ingot is smelted in where no tree is in sight (#729, `makeCostIsNoneWhereTheFurnacesRawItemsHaveNoSource`). A log of one kind the item's own steps take is costed as any log, as `get` first gets any log, unless logs are within `radius` but none of that kind and the plan made with logs of another kind carried still asks for it: then it is that kind's (`logs_of`), and `make_cost` is `None` where no chest holds it, as `get` then fails `no_logs` for it, so oak slabs with only birch trees about cost `None` and sticks the birch (#938, `makeCostOfOakSlabsWithOnlyBirchAroundIsNone`).

Its `cost(n, radius, start=None)` is a tuple `(ticks, tier)`, a finder: `ticks` is `estimate` for each of `n`, and the trip `deep(y)` once more where none of `ore` is exposed within `radius` blocks, so [`lib.dig.ore`](dig.md) would dig stairs down to `y`, branch and climb back: once for the trip, however many it gets. The trip is measured: with a stone pickaxe through stone some 48 ticks a step down (`STEP_DOWN`), 37 a block of branch (`BRANCH`) and 10 a step back up (`STEP_UP`), for the steps from the bot's feet down to `y` and one branch of 32 ([`lib.dig.LENGTH`](dig.md)). The ore sources take `y` from [`lib.dig.best_y`](dig.md), so from sea level that is about 1200 for coal (96: no stairs), 4000 for iron (16) and 8300 for diamond (-59), and diamond costs more than coal from the same height (#564). With coal's 1200, up to 4 coal (16 torches) are charcoal from logs where no coal ore is in sight, and more are dug for. Where one is exposed, its vein ([`ore_vein`](../../mining/ore_vein.md)), up to `n`, costs [`lib.cost.walk`](cost.md) to the nearest exposed block, from the bot or from `cost(n, radius, start)`'s `start` (`make_cost` passes the chest `get` takes the rest from), and the mining of each, [`breaking`](cost.md) an `"ore"` with the best pickaxe carried where it drops the ore, else with that `tier`, as `lib.dig.ore` mines a vein from where it stands; the rest of `n` costs `estimate` each, which counts a walk. So a far vein costs its walk, not a flat amount (#675): coal ore 42 blocks off costs 233 ticks, and with smelting cut to 50 ticks charcoal from two logs beats it (`aFarCoalVeinDoesNotBeatCheaperCharcoal`). Three raw iron from a vein of three cost 99 ticks (a walk of 30 and 23 each) and took some 85 (#661, `makeCostOfAVeinOfRawIronIsCloseToItsTicks`); ponytail: only the nearest vein counts. A source with an `ore` and no `y`, such as one a script adds, costs `DEEP` (2000); `tier` is the pickaxe tier [`lib.tools.tier_for`](tools.md#tier_forwhat) the nearest exposed block of `ore` needs, where the source takes a pickaxe and one is exposed, else the source's `tier`. `get` holds a tool of that tier, and `options` costs one. Its `least(n)` is what `cost` never goes under, without the ore scan, for `make_cost`'s limit: `estimate` for each, or for a source with an `ore`, where that is less, the mining of each with the fastest pickaxe tier that drops it (#681).

## `wood_for_coal(k, radius)`

How many logs `get` gets instead of `k` coal, less the coal the chests of [`lib.stock`](stock.md) within `radius` hold (#564): that many to smelt into charcoal and two thirds as many to burn doing it, which as planks also burn as long as the coal would. `0` where the chests hold all `k`, or the coal source's `cost` for the rest is less than the logs' plus `SMELT` for each charcoal. A finder, done at once. With coal ore exposed within `radius` the coal source wins at any `k` (40 an ore, the rest of its vein 23, against some 310 a charcoal): 8 torches from a vein of two coal cost 80 and took some 70 ticks, from an oak's logs as charcoal 620 and some 740 (#663, `coalFromAVeinInSightBeatsCharcoal`).

## `key(item)`

The item id `get` and `SOURCES` use for `item`: the full id, `"minecraft:"` added where it has no namespace, and `"#minecraft:logs"` for a log without a source of its own. Done at once.
